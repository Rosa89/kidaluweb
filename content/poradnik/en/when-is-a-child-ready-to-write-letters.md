"My child is four and still does not write" is one of the most common worries we hear from parents. Yet writing letters is the last stage of a long road that starts with play dough and hanging laundry on a line. This article will help you recognise which stage your child is at and suggest what to do at each one.

## Writing starts with the hand, not with letters

To write the letter "a", a child has to do several things at once: hold the tool in their fingers, control the pressure, guide the line in the planned direction, stop it in the right place, and at the same time remember what the letter looks like. Each of these skills matures separately.

That is why children who do a lot of modelling, drawing, threading beads and cutting with scissors usually find it easier to start writing. Not because they are more gifted, but because their hand is already prepared.

## Signs that a child is ready

There is no single date. Most children show readiness between the ages of five and seven, but the spread is wide and natural. Instead of looking at the birth certificate, watch for:

- **Grip.** The child holds a crayon with three fingers, not the whole fist. The movement comes from the fingers and wrist, not the whole arm.
- **Drawing.** They can draw a circle, a cross and a square, and a recognisable person with a head, body and limbs.
- **Interest.** They ask "what does it say here", try to "write" their own scribbles and sign their drawings.
- **Dominant hand.** They have already chosen the hand they draw with and do not switch it mid-task.
- **Attention.** They stay with one task for a few minutes without breaking off.

If most of these are already working, you can begin. If not, it is worth going back to hand exercises rather than forcing letters.

## Exercises that prepare for writing

You do not need workbooks or worksheets. The best exercises are the ones the child takes for play:

1. **Play dough and modelling clay.** Kneading, rolling, pinching off small pieces. Builds finger strength.
2. **Threading and clipping.** Beads on a string, clothes pegs on the edge of a box. Trains the pincer grip, the same one used to hold a pencil.
3. **Drawing big shapes.** Chalk on the pavement, a finger on a steamed-up window, a brush on a large sheet. The movement of the whole arm must be free before the fine movement of the fingers takes over.
4. **Tracing with a finger.** Letters cut from sandpaper, sprinkled from semolina, written in a tray of sand. The child follows the shape with a finger and remembers the movement with the body, not just the eyes.
5. **Mazes and dot-to-dot.** Teach guiding a line in a planned direction and stopping it.

## Finger on the screen before pencil on paper

Tracing letters with a finger is a natural in-between stage. It does not yet require pressure control or a precise grip, but it already teaches the most important thing: the order and direction of the strokes that make up a letter. A child who has learned that "a" starts at the top and goes to the left will later repeat the same movement with a pencil.

A well-designed exercise shows the starting point, guides along the trace and reacts when the child strays off the path. It matters that the letter shape matches the one taught at school. Poland has its own school script with characteristic loops, Germany and Austria have different local models. An app that shows letters in a random typeface can fix movements that will later have to be unlearned at school.

## What not to do

- **Do not sit the child down with a lined notebook if they cannot yet draw a circle.** Practising beyond their ability only teaches that writing is hard and unpleasant.
- **Do not force the grip.** Instead, offer thick crayons and short pieces of chalk that make a good grip happen on their own.
- **Do not compare with what a child "should" be able to do at this age.** Development charts describe an average, not your child.

If a child is six, avoids drawing and the grip is still a fist, it is worth talking to a teacher at nursery. Not to look for a problem, but to pick the exercises that will help fastest.
